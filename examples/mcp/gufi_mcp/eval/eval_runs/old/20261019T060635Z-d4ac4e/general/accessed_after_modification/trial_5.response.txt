How you define "changed" matters a lot here:
- **Measured against status change time (ctime)**, which is what "changed" means for Unix files and covers both content and metadata changes: **0 files**.
- **Measured against content modification time (mtime)**: **all 9,989 files** have been accessed since their contents were last modified.

So every file was read after its contents were last written, but each file's ctime is at or after its last read, meaning some later change touched every file. A metadata change such as a permission update or a re-copy would do that. It could also happen if the index was built from copied or restored data.
